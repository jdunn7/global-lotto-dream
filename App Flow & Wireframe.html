Commissions, tiers & the downline

PlayLottoGlobal runs a 3-level (uni-level) commission structure. You earn on the people you recruit, the people they recruit, and one level deeper — three generations of "downline." Your tier sets the rates. Here's Amara's real network: 139 members across three levels.

The downline, three levels deep

YOU — Amaracode AMARA777 · Gold tier
L1 14 direct referrals — people you personally invited 8%
14 directsyour recruits
L2 39 members — recruited by your directs 4%
39 membersyour team's recruits
L3 86 members — recruited by your Level 2 2%
86 membersthird generation
14 + 39 + 86 = 139 in the network. You earn commission on the play volume of all three levels — but the rate shrinks each level down, so width near the top (lots of directs) pays far more than depth.

Margins, overhead & spending discipline

Growth only matters if the math underneath it works. The whole model rests on a simple discipline: sell & retain more tickets — don't buy growth by overspending. Every marketing dollar has to come back as more ticket margin than it cost, or it's just burning runway.

Where every $100 of ticket sales goes

A representative split. Most of a ticket funds prizes; what's left after commissions and processing is the gross margin that has to cover marketing and overhead — and still leave profit.

Prizes $50
Comm. $12

Gross margin $30
$50 · prize pool & winnings reserve $12 · affiliate commissions (blended) $3 · payment processing $5 · tech, API & infra $30 · gross margin (before marketing & overhead)
Illustrative ratios, not booked figures. The headline: roughly $30 of contribution per $100 sold is the pool that pays for acquisition and fixed costs. Keep CAC + overhead per $100 under $30 and the brand prints profit; go over and volume just accelerates losses.

Sell more tickets — don't overspend to fake it

The classic failure mode is buying vanity growth: pouring cash into ads that don't pay back, mistaking spend for traction. The fleet exists to grow cheaply and measurably — every channel earns its budget.

✓ Spend that compounds
  • Channels where LTV > CAC — scale only the winners
  • Owned reach: SEO, content, affiliates, AI influencers (near-zero marginal cost)
  • Retention & reactivation — cheaper than net-new every time
  • Ad spend throttled up after ROAS is proven, not before
✕ Spend that bleeds
  • Blanket ad budgets with no payback tracking
  • Chasing impressions & followers that never convert
  • Discounting margin away to inflate ticket counts
  • Fixed agency retainers that don't flex with results
↗

The lever is volume × retention, not budget. Because gross margin per ticket is fixed, profit grows by selling more tickets to players who come back — not by spending more to acquire one-time buyers. The fleet's job is to make acquisition cheaper over time as memory and persona-targeting sharpen, so each new player costs less than the last.
